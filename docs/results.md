# Results

November 2026. Every number here is regenerated by the commands in the README ("Reproducing the
results"); the figures are written to `results/` and are not in git. **All figures, each with a
caption explaining what it shows and why, are collected in `results/figures/index.html`.**

**Setup.** 24-PRB carrier, 30 kHz SCS, PUSCH with QPSK and LDPC (MCS 5), one front-loaded DMRS
symbol, random allocation of 6–24 PRBs, TDL-A (100 ns) and TDL-C (300 ns), up to 3 m/s. Gaussian
jammer on a block of 2–12 PRBs anywhere on the carrier. JSR is per jammed resource element,
relative to the UE's data-RE energy (DMRS REs carry 3 dB more). Training: 40 000 slots; validation:
5 000; test: 31 000 (grid over JSR −15…+9 dB, SNR drawn from 5–25 dB); scheduling-aware test:
16 200. All splits use separate seed ranges.

**Reference run (sections 3, 4 and 6):** `full_run --seeds 0 1 2` on a Colab T4 GPU through the
Colab CLI: datasets generated on the GPU, each U-Net variant trained with three seeds (9 minutes
for data, training and evaluation), every seed evaluated with its own validation thresholds, then
the recovery experiment and the figures. Detection values are **mean ± standard deviation over
the three seeds**. Sections 1, 2 and 5 were run on a MacBook Pro M1 Pro CPU.

## 1. What each attack does to the link

`results/ber_bler/ber_bler_{A,C}.pdf`. SNR 15 dB, the jammer covers the whole allocation.

| Attack | 50% BLER, TDL-A: JSR / slot energy ratio | 50% BLER, TDL-C: JSR / slot energy ratio |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pilot | +4.1 dB / **−7.0 dB** | +3.7 dB / **−7.4 dB** |
| Data | −2.7 dB / −2.7 dB | −3.2 dB / −3.2 dB |
| Partial-band | −3.7 dB / −3.4 dB | −4.0 dB / −3.6 dB |

Per resource element, pilot jamming needs a higher JSR, but it only occupies 1 of 14 symbols. Per
unit of jammer energy it is the most efficient attack: it reaches 50% BLER with about 4 dB less
energy than data or partial-band jamming, in line with Clancy's pilot-jamming result. Without a
jammer the BLER is 0 at this SNR. Points below BLER ≈ 2·10⁻³ rest on one or two block errors
(1 280 slots per point).

## 2. What the features see

`results/feature_maps.pdf`, and the explanatory figures `residual_explained`, `constellations` and
`feature_distributions`. Mean values over the scheduled cells, JSR 0 dB, SNR 20 dB, jammer on the
whole allocation (linear, noise-normalized):

| Condition | DMRS residual R | EVM E (data cells) | Power P |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| No jammer | 1.0 | 1.3 | 105 |
| Pilot | **108** | 15 (wrong symbols) | 105 (high only on the DMRS row) |
| Data | 1.0 (blind) | **56** | **193** |
| Partial-band | **108** | **66** | **200** |

This matches Table 2 of the proposal. Pilot jamming shows up in the EVM of the *data* symbols,
which is why EVM alone reports it at the wrong symbols. Two corrections to Table 2: without
jamming, normalized EVM is about 1 only at moderate SNR (about 2 at 25 dB and 4 at 30 dB on TDL-C,
because the LS estimator's error variance leaves out interpolation error and channel aging; issue
#6), and the residual leaks into the PRBs next to a jammed block (up to about 6 dB within two
PRBs). The residual of a clean slot stays at 1.0 at every SNR and on every PRB.

By SNR (overall F1, `test_snr5-15_*` and `test_snr15-25_*`): P+R+E 0.916 / 0.987, P+E
0.914 / 0.979, power only 0.867 / 0.959, EVM only 0.707 / 0.834 for SNR 5–15 dB / 15–25 dB.

**Reading** (illustrated by `detector_outputs` and `feature_distributions`).
- The DMRS residual is what makes weak pilot jamming detectable: at −15 dB it raises Pd from
  0.43 (P+E) to 0.62, and pilot-attack F1 from 0.757 to 0.834.
- For data and partial-band jamming, P+R+E and P+E are equal, as expected: the residual cannot
  see an attack that leaves the DMRS alone.
- Each threshold baseline sees only half of the problem: the EVM threshold cannot score the DMRS
  symbol (and flags data cells of pilot-jammed PRBs instead), and the residual threshold is blind
  to data jamming.

## 4. Robustness: scheduling-aware jammer

`results/eval/sched_pd_vs_jsr_scheduled.pdf`. The jammer hits exactly the scheduled PRBs. No
U-Net saw this attacker during training.

| Detector | F1 pilot | F1 data | F1 all | Pd pilot, −15 dB | Pd data, −15 dB |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **U-Net, P+R+E** | **0.884 ± 0.014** | 0.927 ± 0.014 | 0.933 ± 0.011 | **0.62 ± 0.01** | 0.52 ± 0.03 |
| U-Net, P+E | 0.791 ± 0.013 | **0.936 ± 0.017** | **0.936 ± 0.014** | 0.36 ± 0.04 | **0.58 ± 0.05** |
| U-Net, power only | 0.817 ± 0.008 | 0.895 ± 0.008 | 0.888 ± 0.009 | 0.21 ± 0.03 | 0.45 ± 0.04 |
| U-Net, EVM only | 0.468 ± 0.032 | 0.696 ± 0.061 | 0.685 ± 0.062 | 0.05 ± 0.01 | 0.19 ± 0.01 |

**Reading.** When the whole allocation is jammed there is no clean neighbouring PRB to compare
against. For **pilot jamming** the fused detector is unaffected (Pd 0.62 at −15 dB in both tests),
because the residual is an absolute measure; without it Pd falls from 0.43 to 0.36. For **data
jamming** every U-Net loses some sensitivity, the fused one slightly more than P+E (overall F1
0.933 vs 0.936, within one standard deviation). The networks were trained only on jammers placed
at random, so part of what they learned is contrast between jammed and unjammed PRBs; adding
scheduling-aware slots to training is the obvious fix, at the cost of no longer testing an
unseen attacker.

## 6. What the map is good for: map-aware recovery

`results/recovery/recovery_{A,C}.pdf`. UE on 12 PRBs, jammer on a 4-PRB block inside the
allocation, SNR 15 dB; every batch decoded by all five receivers (1 280 slots per point). The
map-aware receiver drops pilots in flagged PRBs, LMMSE-interpolates the channel from the clean
ones (same channel model as the residual) and erases the soft bits of flagged data cells.

BLER, TDL-C (TDL-A is similar, slightly easier):

| Case | JSR | No protection | LMMSE, no map | Map P+E | **Map P+R+E** | True map |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| No jammer | – | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.001 | 0.002 | 0.000 |
| Pilot | −9 dB | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.109 | 0.060 | 0.000 |
| Pilot | 0 dB | 0.010 | 0.003 | 0.036 | 0.011 | 0.000 |
| Pilot | +9 dB | 0.313 | 0.154 | 0.003 | **0.004** | 0.001 |
| Data | −9 dB | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.037 | 0.050 | 0.000 |
| Data | 0 dB | 0.174 | 0.179 | 0.011 | **0.008** | 0.002 |
| Data | +9 dB | 0.800 | 0.811 | 0.003 | **0.004** | 0.003 |
| Partial | 0 dB | 0.288 | 0.230 | 0.009 | **0.005** | 0.002 |
| Partial | +9 dB | 0.888 | 0.867 | 0.006 | **0.005** | 0.005 |
| Whole-allocation pilot | 0 dB | 0.105 | – | – | – | 0.105 |

**Reading.**
- **Strong jamming:** the map turns a broken link into a working one. Data jamming at +9 dB: BLER
  0.80 → 0.004; pilot jamming: 0.31 → 0.004. The detectors' maps come close to the true map.
- **The LMMSE estimate alone** helps against pilot jamming (0.31 → 0.15), because it averages over
  many pilots; it does nothing against data jamming. The map is what removes the rest.
- **The residual matters for recovery too:** against pilot jamming the P+R+E map beats the P+E
  map at every JSR below +9 dB (e.g. 0.060 vs 0.109 at −9 dB), because it localizes the jammed
  DMRS cells better.
- **Weak jamming has a price.** Below about −6 dB (data, partial) or 0 dB (pilot) the unprotected
  link already decodes, and the detector's false alarms then erase clean data or drop clean
  pilots: BLER up to 0.06 where no protection gives 0. Without any jammer the cost is small
  (0.002). In practice the protection should only be switched on when the detector reports a
  substantial jammed area; that gating is not part of this study.
- **Limit case:** when every pilot of the allocation is jammed, nothing can be repaired, and the
  map-aware receiver behaves exactly like the unprotected one (0.105 vs 0.105), as designed.

## Caveats before these go into the paper

- Each Pd point averages over SNR 5–25 dB; the SNR-range files give the split by SNR.
- The 1% false-alarm threshold is set over all non-jammed cells a detector scores; inside the
  allocation the fused U-Net's false-alarm rate is somewhat higher (about 1.6% on local checks).
- 15 training epochs per run; the validation loss is lowest well before that (training curves).
- Only 190 different allocations exist, so test allocations repeat training ones; channel
  realizations and jammer draws do not.
- The reference datasets were generated on a GPU; GPU and CPU draw different random numbers, so
  results are reproducible per device, not bit-identical across devices.
- Normalized EVM is biased upward at high SNR without jamming (issue #6); the detectors are
  calibrated on data that contains this effect.
- On Colab's CUDA 13 build, PyTorch's batched complex double-precision solve (MAGMA) crashes; the
  LMMSE interpolation therefore solves its small systems on the CPU.
