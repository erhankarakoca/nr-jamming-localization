# Results

November 2026. Every number here is regenerated by the commands in the README ("Reproducing the
results"); the figures are written to `results/` and are not in git.

**Setup.** 24-PRB carrier, 30 kHz SCS, PUSCH with QPSK and LDPC (MCS 5), one front-loaded DMRS
symbol, random allocation of 6–24 PRBs, TDL-A (100 ns) and TDL-C (300 ns), up to 3 m/s. Gaussian
jammer on a block of 2–12 PRBs anywhere on the carrier. JSR is per jammed resource element,
relative to the UE's data-RE energy (DMRS REs carry 3 dB more). Training: 40 000 slots; validation:
5 000; test: 31 000 (grid over JSR −15…+9 dB, SNR drawn from 5–25 dB); scheduling-aware test:
16 200. All splits use separate seed ranges.

**Detection runs (sections 3–4):** `full_run --seeds 0 1 2` on a Colab T4 GPU (7 min in total):
datasets generated on the GPU, each U-Net variant trained with three seeds, and every seed
evaluated with its own validation thresholds. Values are **mean ± standard deviation over the
three seeds**. Sections 1, 2 and 5 were run on a MacBook Pro M1 Pro CPU.

## 1. What each attack does to the link

`results/ber_bler/ber_bler_{A,C}.pdf`. SNR 15 dB, the jammer covers the whole allocation.

| Attack | 50% BLER, TDL-A: JSR / slot energy ratio | 50% BLER, TDL-C: JSR / slot energy ratio |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pilot | +4.1 dB / **−7.0 dB** | +3.7 dB / **−7.4 dB** |
| Data | −2.7 dB / −2.7 dB | −3.2 dB / −3.2 dB |
| Partial-band | −3.7 dB / −3.4 dB | −4.0 dB / −3.6 dB |

Per resource element, pilot jamming needs a higher JSR, but it only occupies 1 of 14 symbols. Per
unit of jammer energy it is the most efficient attack: it reaches 50% BLER with about 4 dB less
energy than data or partial-band jamming, in line with Clancy's pilot-jamming result. Without a
jammer the BLER is 0 at this SNR. Points below BLER ≈ 2·10⁻³ rest on one or two block errors
(1 280 slots per point).

## 2. What the features see

`results/feature_maps.pdf`. Mean values over the scheduled cells, JSR 0 dB, SNR 20 dB, jammer on
the whole allocation (linear, noise-normalized):

| Condition | DMRS residual R | EVM E (data cells) | Power P |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| No jammer | 1.0 | 1.3 | 105 |
| Pilot | **108** | 15 (wrong symbols) | 105 (high only on the DMRS row) |
| Data | 1.0 (blind) | **56** | **193** |
| Partial-band | **108** | **66** | **200** |

This matches Table 2 of the proposal. Pilot jamming shows up in the EVM of the *data* symbols,
which is why EVM alone reports it at the wrong symbols. One correction to Table 2: without
jamming, normalized EVM is about 1 only at moderate SNR. It rises to about 2 at 25 dB and about 4
at 30 dB on TDL-C, because the LS estimator's error variance leaves out interpolation error and
channel aging (issue #6). The residual stays at 1.0 at every SNR and on every PRB, because the
UE's own channel lies inside the delay subspace.

By SNR (overall F1, `test_snr5-15_*` and `test_snr15-25_*`): P+R+E 0.920 / 0.986, P+E
0.915 / 0.976, power only 0.869 / 0.960, EVM only 0.709 / 0.831 for SNR 5–15 dB / 15–25 dB.

**Reading.**
- The DMRS residual is what makes weak pilot jamming detectable: at −15 dB it raises Pd from
  0.39 ± 0.05 (P+E) to 0.62, and pilot-attack F1 from 0.716 to 0.833. The overall F1 gap
  (0.953 vs 0.946) is small, but it is seven standard deviations and holds for every seed.
- For data and partial-band jamming, P+R+E and P+E are equal, as expected: the residual cannot
  see an attack that leaves the DMRS alone.
- Each threshold baseline sees only half of the problem: the EVM threshold cannot score the DMRS
  symbol, and the residual threshold is blind to data jamming.

## 4. Robustness: scheduling-aware jammer

`results/eval/sched_pd_vs_jsr_scheduled.pdf`. The jammer hits exactly the scheduled PRBs. No
U-Net saw this attacker during training.

| Detector | F1 pilot | F1 data | F1 all | Pd pilot, −15 dB | Pd data, −15 dB |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **U-Net, P+R+E** | **0.887 ± 0.017** | 0.928 ± 0.010 | 0.932 ± 0.007 | **0.61 ± 0.01** | 0.55 ± 0.02 |
| U-Net, P+E | 0.736 ± 0.030 | **0.946 ± 0.006** | **0.939 ± 0.003** | 0.27 ± 0.05 | **0.63 ± 0.02** |
| U-Net, power only | 0.818 ± 0.003 | 0.902 ± 0.003 | 0.893 ± 0.004 | 0.18 ± 0.01 | 0.49 ± 0.01 |
| U-Net, EVM only | 0.457 ± 0.095 | 0.658 ± 0.089 | 0.649 ± 0.095 | 0.07 ± 0.02 | 0.20 ± 0.05 |

**Reading.** When the whole allocation is jammed, there is no clean neighbouring PRB to compare
against.
- **Pilot jamming:** the fused detector is unaffected (Pd 0.62 → 0.61), because the residual is
  an absolute measure. Without it, Pd falls from 0.39 to 0.27.
- **Data jamming:** every U-Net loses some sensitivity. The fused one loses a little more than
  P+E (data F1 0.959 → 0.928, against 0.955 → 0.946). The EVM-only network largely breaks down:
  its Pd even drops at high JSR (0.54 ± 0.20 at +3 dB, against 0.71 at −3 dB).

The networks were trained only on jammers placed at random, so part of what they learned is
contrast between jammed and unjammed PRBs. Adding scheduling-aware slots to training is the
obvious fix, at the cost of no longer testing an unseen attacker.

The threshold baselines flag many non-jammed cells in this split (EVM threshold: 8.9% of the cells
it scores). That is the wrong-symbol effect again: pilot jamming of the whole allocation raises
the EVM of every data cell, and those cells are not jammed.

With PyTorch's default eight threads the U-Net takes about 1.3 ms; for an input this small the
thread pool only adds overhead.

## Caveats before these go into the paper

- Each Pd point averages over SNR 5–25 dB; the SNR-range files give the split by SNR.
- 15 training epochs per run (validation loss flattened after about 8–12 in earlier runs).
- Only 190 different allocations exist, so test allocations repeat training ones; channel
  realizations and jammer draws do not.
- The datasets behind sections 3–4 were generated on a GPU. GPU and CPU draw different random
  numbers, so they are reproducible per device, not bit-identical across devices.
- Normalized EVM is biased upward at high SNR without jamming (issue #6); the detectors are
  calibrated on data that contains this effect.
