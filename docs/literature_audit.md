# Novelty check and related work for NR uplink jamming localization

Review date and search cutoff: **17 November 2026**. Items are referred to by their keys in `docs/references.bib`; the first 45 audited items come from the author's Zotero collection, the rest from a separate database and web search.

## A. Novelty verdict and the five closest works

**Prominent prior-art warning: C1 is not new as a general resource-localization task, and much of C4 already exists.** Li et al. (2012, **`li2012robust`**) detect corrupted tones in each OFDM symbol, erase corrupted pilots, interpolate from the remaining pilots, and select interference-aware LLR rules before LDPC decoding. Örnek and Kartal (2024, **`ornek2024advanced`**) explicitly propose a **Symbol-Resource Block-EVM** representation for time-frequency jamming detection. Neither a two-dimensional resource representation nor “detect, exclude pilots, and protect soft decoding” is a defensible standalone first claim. [Li et al., primary MERL record](https://www.merl.com/publications/TR2012-084), [Örnek–Kartal, author institution abstract](https://research.itu.edu.tr/en/publications/an-advanced-evm-metric-based-jamming-signal-detection-method-for-/).

**Overall assessment:** the supplied system has a plausible **specific combination and empirical contribution**, rather than four independently new ideas. The strongest angle is receiver-internal feature fusion that localizes weak pilot attacks at their injection support despite nonlocal EVM impairment, followed by evaluation of how that map changes NR channel estimation and decoding. No inspected source establishes the complete C1-C4 combination. That is a bounded search finding, not a proof of priority; key full-text gaps remain `mehrabian2026robust` and `ornek2024advanced`.

### Five closest works, ranked for this paper

1. **`li2012robust` — Liangbin Li, Ramesh Annavajjala, Toshiaki Koike-Akino, and Philip Orlik, “Robust receiver algorithms to mitigate partial-band and partial-time interference in LDPC-coded OFDM systems,” MILCOM 2012, pp. 1–6.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/MILCOM.2012.6415663). **Strong C1/C4 overlap.** Section III-A constructs a corrupted-tone index set for each symbol using a false-alarm-constrained power test; III-B erases flagged pilots and uses clean-pilot LS/linear interpolation; III-C/Eqs. (4)–(8) accounts for channel-estimation error and switches LLR rules on flagged data tones. Your difference is weak-pilot-aware learned NR support estimation, PRB-level exclusion, LMMSE interpolation with a frequency-dependent error variance and zero-LLR erasure, rather than their strong-interference detector, linear interpolation and robust/dynamic LLRs. The local MERL PDF has two cover pages: paper pp. 3–4 are PDF pp. 5–6. The paper itself notes that Wiener interpolation is available when the PDP is known, so replacing linear interpolation with LMMSE is a standard refinement, not a new architectural principle.

2. **`ornek2024advanced` — Cem Örnek and Mesut Kartal, “An Advanced EVM Metric Based Jamming Signal Detection Method for Next-Generation Communication Systems,” BlackSeaCom 2024, pp. 308–311.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/BlackSeaCom61746.2024.10646255). **Strong C1/C2 overlap at representation/measurement level.** The institutional abstract expressly describes Symbol-RB-EVM localization in both time and frequency, including use as an alternative to spectrogram inputs for ML. Your differences are the binary injection-support target, residual-assisted handling of pilot contamination, effective-variance-normalized decision-directed EVM and the evaluated receiver action; **full text was not obtained**, so its exact decision rule, labels and pilot-attack experiments must remain unresolved. Its 2023 journal predecessor `ornek2023securing` also makes EVM-versus-RB localization and weak-jammer detection explicit.

3. **`mehrabian2026robust` — Amir Mehrabian and Georges Kaddoum, “Robust Jamming Detection for Weak and Strong Signal Powers Using Pilot Subspace Projection,” IEEE Communications Letters, vol. 30, pp. 2168–2172, 2026.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/LCOMM.2026.3697967). **Strong C2 conceptual overlap, exact implementation overlap unresolved.** The publisher abstract establishes multi-antenna pilot-subspace detectors R1 and R1-CFAR and robustness to weak legitimate-signal power. Your described residual is a SISO frequency/delay channel-subspace residual used as a spatially indexed feature, fused with power/EVM to estimate support; the abstract does **not** establish that `mehrabian2026robust` uses your uniform-PDP/Slepian construction. Because its full text was unavailable, describe your baseline as “inspired by pilot-subspace projection” unless you can verify faithful reproduction. [Primary institutional record](https://pure.etsmtl.ca/en/publications/robust-jamming-detection-for-weak-and-strong-signal-powers-using-/).

4. **`kavvousanos2026deep` — Emmanouil Kavvousanos, Francky Catthoor, and Vassilis Paliouras, “Deep Learning for Joint Narrowband Interference Cancellation and Soft Demodulation in OFDM Systems,” arXiv:2607.08717, 2026, preprint.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2607.08717). **Strong C3/C4 constituent overlap.** Its 14,468-parameter NBI-CNet estimates/cancels per-tone interference and a 692-parameter LLR-CNet fuses cleaned observations, estimated interference and noise variance to output soft bits; Fig. 7 also compares an LLR-erasure/blanking baseline using windows around detected interferers. Your difference is an explicit PRB-by-symbol support mask and pilot-contaminated TDL channel recovery: this predecessor assumes the desired channel is AWGN with `H = 1` and uses asynchronous multitone cancellation rather than clean-pilot LMMSE reconstruction. It also shows why 66k parameters or neural interference-to-soft-decoding processing alone is not new; compare timing only with matched hardware and batch size.

5. **`wang2026downlink` — Shao-Di Wang, Chang-Long Wang, Hui-Ming Wang, Feng Zhou, and Victor C. M. Leung, “Downlink Control Information Sniffing-Based Smart Jamming and Its Suppression Strategy in 5G NR,” IEEE Transactions on Information Forensics and Security, vol. 21, pp. 4021–4034, 2026.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIFS.2026.3683183). **Close attack model and partial C1/C4 overlap.** It targets PUSCH using decoded scheduling information and suppresses attacks through spatial-feature-based scheduled-subset identification and resource reconstruction. Your distinction is SISO resource-support inference from receiver features and pilot/LLR processing rather than spatial separation; scheduling-aware jamming itself is established here and experimentally in `flores2023implementation`/`alaimo2025exploiting`. This comparison rests on the primary abstract, not a verified full derivation. [IEEE abstract](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/11480180).

Other mandatory distinctions: **`reusmuns2024senseoran`** already does learned time-frequency interferer localization; **`neasamonisanthi2025interfo`** already integrates IQ/KPI feature fusion for real-time NR uplink interference detection, but outputs presence only and leaves mitigation to future work; **`honkala2021deeprx` DeepRx** already fuses received grids, pilot references and raw channel estimates to directly predict soft bits in an NR uplink. Newly found **`tang2025interference`** applies learned masks to SRS-derived channel/sensing reconstruction, while **`ulbricht2024pyjama` PyJama** is a very close Sionna/OFDM jamming-model antecedent. These are useful additional comparisons, even though no single one matches the full C1-C4 combination.

### Suggested claim wording and citation budget

A supportable formulation is: “We investigate receiver-internal feature fusion for PRB-by-symbol localization of resource-selective jamming in a SISO NR uplink. A delay-constrained DMRS residual complements decision-directed EVM when pilot contamination displaces impairment into unjammed data symbols. We evaluate how the inferred support guides pilot exclusion, uncertainty-aware channel reconstruction and data-bit erasure.” Avoid “first jamming map,” “first U-Net jammer detector,” “first pilot exclusion/LLR protection receiver” and a new scheduling-aware attack claim.

For 5–6 pages, prioritize **`li2012robust`, `ornek2024advanced` (with `ornek2023securing` or `ornek2022efficient` for the baseline), `mehrabian2026robust`, `honkala2021deeprx`, `wang2026downlink` or `flores2023implementation`, `reusmuns2024senseoran`, `neasamonisanthi2025interfo`, `kavvousanos2026deep`, `wang2023sequential`, `ulbricht2024pyjama`, `kumar2019sdr`, `tang2025interference` and `rajchowski2024examination`**, plus **`gpp20265g`/`hoydis2022sionna`** for NR and Sionna. Other entries below are optional context, not a request to cite the whole audit library. Use the actual standard/software versions used by the experiments rather than silently adopting the newest collected version.

Three reporting points matter to these claims: (i) call 24 × 14 **PRB-by-symbol**, not resource-element resolution; (ii) a 30 kHz normal-CP NR slot lasts **0.5 ms**, so **0.8 ms on one core does not demonstrate sustained every-slot real-time throughput**; (iii) the stated BLER improvement is an author-supplied result, not independently reproduced here. Report whether it uses estimated or oracle maps, and include interpolation-only/erasure-only ablations and performance when no clean pilots remain. The NR timing follows the numerology in [ETSI TS 138 211 V18.10.0](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_ts/138200_138299/138211/18.10.00_60/ts_138211v181000p.pdf).

A technical inference from the stated residual model: with a truncated subspace, the residual contains channel-model leakage as well as interference and noise, and the projection can spread localized interference across pilot indices. Its projected-noise covariance is proportional to `I − U Uᴴ`, so normalization, retained rank and `tau_max` mismatch deserve explicit specification and held-out calibration; the residual is not guaranteed to equal pure interference plus noise under every TDL realization. This does not negate the contribution, but bounds the physical interpretation of the feature.

### Searched but no verified exact match found

These are **negative results for the inspected accessible literature**, not claims that no such work exists:

- A **SISO NR PUSCH** learned binary injection-support map at PRB-by-symbol resolution that explicitly tests **pilot-only contamination with EVM impairment displaced into other symbols**, rather than only classifying an attack or locating a spectral region. Symbol/RB detection itself was found in `ornek2024advanced`, and per-tone/per-symbol support in `li2012robust`.
- The exact **three-feature construction**: noise-normalized received power, a raw-LS DMRS residual after a **uniform-PDP finite-delay/Slepian channel-subspace projection**, and decision-directed EVM divided by the LMMSE effective noise variance, fused into a support-segmentation network. General projection, EVM, low-rank separation and model-informed learning were all found.
- An implemented receiver that takes **that inferred map** and jointly performs **PRB-level pilot exclusion, clean-pilot frequency LMMSE interpolation with per-subcarrier posterior error variance, and data-cell zero-LLR erasure**, evaluated on coded NR BLER. Most of the general detection/pilot-erasure/soft-decoding architecture was found in `li2012robust`; `rajchowski2024examination` proposes related NR channel-estimation feedback as future work.
- A reported **matched low-per-cell-FAR support comparison** showing specifically that a delay-residual feature recovers weak pilot attacks while EVM localizes their downstream damage incorrectly. Prior EVM work already detects weak jamming at zero BER, so weak-attack detection or attack-versus-damage motivation alone is not new.
- An O-RAN xApp/dApp implementing this exact within-slot map and pilot/LLR chain. Existing O-RAN detection, TF localization, operational adaptation and real-time IQ/KPI fusion were found and should be cited.
